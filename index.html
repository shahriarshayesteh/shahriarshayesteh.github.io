Research

AI increasingly sits between people and other systems.

In that position, AI does more than generate language. It helps decide what information passes through. I study those decisions in two directions: what AI agents send when acting for users, and what LLMs leave out when explaining complex information.

01

Agentic AI privacy

When AI speaks for us, what gets sent?

AI agents increasingly communicate with external services on users' behalf. I study how tool interfaces shape what personal information agents can include in those requests, and when interface design creates opportunities for unnecessary disclosure.

2,344 tool schemas audited · PrivateNLP @ ACL 2026

02

LLM content selection

When AI explains things to us, what gets left out?

LLMs can make long consumer contracts much easier to understand, but a clear and accurate explanation can still omit consequential terms or lose important conditions and exceptions. I audit what these systems emphasize, underemphasize, and leave out, comparing their explanations with contract structure, what consumers discuss publicly, and legally consequential mechanisms grounded in prior legal scholarship.

Ongoing research · Consumer contracts · Legal NLP

Across both directions, I use black-box auditing, controlled experiments, human annotation, and source-grounded evaluation.

About

About Shahriar.

Shahriar Shayesteh is a PhD candidate in Informatics at Penn State, working with Shomir Wilson in the Human Language Technologies Lab. His research focuses on auditing how LLMs and AI agents select and disclose information. His current work examines privacy in tool-using agents and content selection in AI explanations of consumer contracts.
